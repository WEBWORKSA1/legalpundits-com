Legal Glossary

122 legal terms explained in plain English.

Term of the day

…

ABCDEFGHIJLMNOPQRSTUVW

A

Acquittal
A finding by a judge or jury that a defendant is not guilty of a criminal charge.
Adjustment of status
The process of applying for a green card from inside the United States.
Admissible evidence
Evidence a court allows the judge or jury to consider.
Affidavit
A written statement of facts signed under oath, often before a notary.
Affirmative defense
A defense that admits the basic facts but offers a legal reason the defendant shouldn't be liable, such as self-defense.
Alimony
Payments from one spouse to the other after separation or divorce; also called spousal support or maintenance.
Answer
The defendant's formal written response to a lawsuit.
Appeal
A request asking a higher court to review a lower court's decision for legal error.
Arbitration
A private process where a neutral arbitrator decides a dispute instead of a court.
Arraignment
The court hearing where a defendant is formally told the charges and enters a plea.
At-will employment
A job either side can end at any time for any lawful reason, or no reason.
Automatic stay
The court order that stops most collection actions as soon as a bankruptcy is filed.

B

Bail
Money or conditions set to secure a defendant's return to court while released before trial.
Bench trial
A trial decided by a judge without a jury.
Beneficiary
A person or organization that receives property or benefits under a will, trust, insurance policy or account.
Beyond a reasonable doubt
The high standard of proof required to convict someone of a crime.
Breach of contract
Failing to perform a promise in a legally binding agreement without a valid excuse.
Burden of proof
The duty to prove a disputed fact; in civil cases usually by a preponderance of the evidence.

C

Capacity
The legal and mental ability to make decisions, sign contracts or make a will.
Cause of action
The legal basis for a lawsuit, such as negligence or breach of contract.
Cease and desist letter
A written demand to stop an activity, such as infringement or harassment, often threatening legal action.
Class action
A lawsuit in which one or a few people sue on behalf of a larger group with similar claims.
Common law
Law developed through court decisions rather than statutes.
Comparative negligence
A rule that reduces an injured person's recovery by their own percentage of fault.
Complaint
The document that starts a civil lawsuit, stating the facts and what the plaintiff wants.
Conservatorship
A court appointment of someone to manage the finances of a person who can't manage them.
Consideration
Something of value exchanged by each side that makes a contract binding.
Contempt of court
Disobeying a court order or disrupting court proceedings, punishable by fines or jail.
Contingency fee
A fee arrangement where the lawyer is paid a percentage of the recovery and nothing if the case is lost.
Contributory negligence
A strict rule in a few jurisdictions that bars recovery if the injured person was even slightly at fault.
Copyright
Legal protection for original creative works such as writing, music, art, video and software.
Counterclaim
A claim a defendant files against the plaintiff in the same lawsuit.

D

Damages
Money awarded to compensate for loss or injury.
Default judgment
A judgment entered because the defendant didn't respond or appear.
Defendant
The person or business being sued or charged with a crime.
Deposition
Sworn out-of-court testimony recorded for use in a lawsuit.
Discharge (bankruptcy)
The court order releasing a debtor from personal liability for certain debts.
Discovery
The pre-trial process where each side obtains evidence from the other.
Discovery rule
A rule that starts a limitations clock when an injury was, or should have been, discovered.
Docket
The official list of filings and events in a court case.
Due process
The constitutional guarantee of fair legal procedures before the government takes life, liberty or property.
Durable power of attorney
A power of attorney that remains effective if the person who signed it becomes incapacitated.

E

Easement
A right to use someone else's land for a specific purpose, such as a driveway or utility line.
Equitable distribution
A system of dividing marital property fairly, but not necessarily equally, in divorce.
Escrow
Money or documents held by a neutral third party until conditions are met.
Estate
Everything a person owns at death, including property, money and debts.
Eviction
The legal process by which a landlord removes a tenant through the courts.
Executor
The person named in a will to manage the estate through probate.
Exempt property
Property a debtor can keep in bankruptcy or protect from creditors under state or federal law.
Expungement
A court process that erases or seals a criminal record.

F

Felony
A serious crime usually punishable by more than one year in prison.
Fiduciary
Someone legally required to act in another person's best interest, such as a trustee or agent.

G

Garnishment
A court-ordered process taking part of a debtor's wages or bank funds to pay a judgment.
Guardian ad litem
A person appointed by a court to represent a child's best interests in a case.

H

Habeas corpus
A court order requiring authorities to justify a person's detention.
Hearsay
An out-of-court statement offered to prove its truth, generally inadmissible subject to many exceptions.

I

Indictment
A formal criminal charge issued by a grand jury.
Injunction
A court order requiring someone to do, or stop doing, something.
Intestate
Dying without a valid will; state law then decides who inherits.

J

Joint and several liability
A rule allowing a plaintiff to collect the full judgment from any one of several responsible defendants.
Judgment
The court's final decision in a case.
Jurisdiction
A court's legal power to hear a particular case or over particular people.

L

Liability
Legal responsibility for a debt, harm or obligation.
Lien
A legal claim against property to secure payment of a debt.
Limited liability company (LLC)
A business entity that protects owners from personal liability for business debts, with flexible taxation.
Litigation
The process of resolving disputes through lawsuits.
Living trust
A trust created during your life to hold assets, often used to avoid probate.
Living will
A document stating your wishes about life-sustaining medical treatment.

M

Malpractice
Professional negligence by a doctor, lawyer or other professional that causes harm.
Mediation
A voluntary process where a neutral mediator helps the parties reach their own settlement.
Miranda warnings
The rights police must give before custodial interrogation, including the right to remain silent and to a lawyer.
Misdemeanor
A less serious crime, usually punishable by up to one year in jail.
Motion
A formal request asking the court to make a ruling or order.

N

Negligence
Failing to use the care a reasonable person would use, causing harm to someone else.
No-fault divorce
A divorce granted without proving either spouse did something wrong.
No-fault insurance
Auto insurance where your own policy pays your injury costs regardless of who caused the crash.
Notary public
An official who witnesses signatures and administers oaths; in the US, not a lawyer.
Notice of claim
A formal notice that must be given, often within a short deadline, before suing a government entity.

O

Objection
A lawyer's formal protest during trial that a question or evidence is improper.

P

Pain and suffering
Compensation for physical pain and emotional distress caused by an injury.
Parole
Supervised early release from prison.
Patent
A government grant giving an inventor exclusive rights to an invention for a limited time.
Plaintiff
The person or business who starts a civil lawsuit.
Plea bargain
An agreement in which a defendant pleads guilty, often to a lesser charge, in exchange for concessions.
Pleading
A formal written statement of a party's claims or defenses, such as a complaint or answer.
Power of attorney
A document authorizing someone to act on your behalf in legal or financial matters.
Precedent
An earlier court decision that guides how later similar cases are decided.
Preponderance of the evidence
The usual civil standard of proof: more likely than not.
Pro bono
Legal work done for free, usually for people who can't afford a lawyer.
Pro se
Representing yourself in court without a lawyer.
Probable cause
A reasonable basis to believe a crime was committed, needed for arrests and most search warrants.
Probate
The court-supervised process of settling a deceased person's estate.
Probation
A sentence allowing a person to remain in the community under court supervision and conditions.
Punitive damages
Damages meant to punish especially reckless or malicious conduct, beyond compensating the victim.

Q

Quitclaim deed
A deed transferring whatever interest the seller has in property, without guaranteeing title.

R

Restraining order
A court order prohibiting someone from contacting or approaching another person.
Retainer
An upfront payment to a lawyer, often held in trust and billed against.
Retaliation
Punishing someone for exercising a legal right, such as complaining about discrimination.

S

Settlement
An agreement resolving a dispute without a final court decision.
Small claims court
A simplified court for money disputes below a set dollar limit.
Standing
The legal right to bring a lawsuit because you were directly affected.
Statute
A written law passed by a legislature.
Statute of limitations
The deadline for filing a lawsuit or bringing criminal charges.
Subpoena
A court order requiring a person to testify or produce documents.
Summary judgment
A ruling deciding a case without trial when there's no genuine dispute about the key facts.
Summons
The official notice telling a defendant they've been sued and must respond.

T

Testimony
Statements made by a witness under oath.
Tolling
Pausing or delaying a statute of limitations.
Tort
A civil wrong, other than breach of contract, that causes harm, such as negligence or defamation.
Trademark
A word, logo or symbol that identifies the source of goods or services.
Trust
An arrangement where a trustee holds property for the benefit of beneficiaries.
Trustee
The person or institution managing a trust or a bankruptcy estate.

U

Unlawful detainer
The name for an eviction lawsuit in many states.

V

Venue
The specific court location where a case is heard.
Verdict
The decision of a jury, or a judge in a bench trial.
Visa Bulletin
The State Department's monthly chart showing which green card applicants can move forward based on priority dates.
Voir dire
Jury selection, where lawyers and the judge question potential jurors.

W

Warrant
A court order authorizing an arrest or a search.
Will
A legal document stating how your property should be distributed and who should manage your estate after death.
Workers' compensation
State insurance providing medical care and wage replacement to employees injured on the job, regardless of fault.
Wrongful death
A lawsuit brought by survivors when someone dies because of another's wrongful act or negligence.
Wrongful termination
Firing an employee for an illegal reason, such as discrimination or retaliation.
