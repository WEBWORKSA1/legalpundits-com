Child Custody Basics: How Courts Decide

Legal versus physical custody, the best-interests standard, parenting plans and how to modify a custody order.

By the LegalPundits Editorial Team · Last reviewed November 2026 · 3 min read · How we write

Key takeaways

  • Courts decide custody based on the child's best interests, not the parents' wishes alone.
  • Legal custody covers decision-making; physical custody covers where the child lives.
  • A detailed parenting plan prevents most future conflict.
  • Custody orders can be modified when circumstances materially change.

Two kinds of custody

  • Legal custody is the right to make major decisions about education, health care and religious upbringing. Joint legal custody is very common.
  • Physical custody decides where the child lives day to day. It can be primary to one parent with visitation (parenting time) for the other, or shared.

The best-interests standard

Every state decides custody by asking what is in the child's best interests. Factors commonly include:

  • Each parent's ability to provide stability, care and a safe home.
  • The child's relationship with each parent and siblings.
  • Each parent's willingness to support the child's relationship with the other parent.
  • The child's adjustment to home, school and community.
  • Any history of domestic violence, abuse, neglect or substance misuse.
  • The child's preferences, given more weight as children mature.

Many states now presume or encourage substantial time with both parents when it is safe.

Parenting plans

A parenting plan sets the regular schedule, holidays, school breaks, transport, communication and how decisions are made. Common schedules include alternating weeks, 2-2-3 and 5-2-2-5 rotations. The more specific the plan, the fewer disputes later.

Relocation

Moving a significant distance with a child usually requires the other parent's consent or court approval, and notice deadlines apply.

Modifying custody

Courts can change custody when there has been a substantial change in circumstances, such as a move, a change in a parent's schedule, safety concerns or the child's changing needs, and the change serves the child's best interests.

Practical tips

Keep communication civil and in writing, follow the current order exactly, keep a calendar of parenting time, and never involve children in adult disputes. Judges notice which parent supports the child's relationship with the other.

Frequently asked questions

At what age can a child choose which parent to live with?

No state lets a child simply choose at a set age, but judges give increasing weight to the preferences of mature children, often from early teens.

Do mothers automatically get custody?

No. Custody laws are gender-neutral and focus on the child's best interests.

What if the other parent ignores the custody order?

You can file a motion to enforce the order or for contempt. Keep a detailed record of violations.

When to call a lawyer

Get a family lawyer if there are safety concerns, the other parent plans to relocate, custody is contested, or you're facing an emergency custody hearing.
