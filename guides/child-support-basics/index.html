Child Support Basics: How It's Calculated and Enforced

How state guidelines calculate child support, what income counts, how to change an order and how unpaid support is collected.

By the LegalPundits Editorial Team · Last reviewed November 2026 · 3 min read · How we write

Key takeaways

  • Every state uses a child support guideline formula; most use an income-shares model.
  • Income, parenting time, health insurance and childcare costs drive the number.
  • Support orders can be modified only by the court; informal agreements don't change what's legally owed.
  • Unpaid support is collected aggressively, including through wage withholding and tax refund intercepts.

How support is calculated

Each state sets a guideline formula. Two main models exist:

  • Income shares (used by most states): the court estimates what both parents would spend on the child if they lived together, then splits that amount according to each parent's share of combined income.
  • Percentage of income: support is a set percentage of the paying parent's income, which rises with the number of children.

Adjustments usually account for the number of overnights each parent has, health insurance premiums, work-related childcare and extraordinary medical or educational expenses. Courts can depart from the guideline in unusual circumstances but must usually explain why.

What counts as income

Wages, salaries, bonuses, commissions, self-employment income, rental income, unemployment and disability benefits commonly count. If a parent is voluntarily unemployed or underemployed, the court can impute income based on earning capacity.

Changing an order

Either parent can ask the court to modify support when circumstances change substantially, such as job loss, a big raise, a change in custody or new needs of the child. Many states use a threshold, such as a change of 10% to 20% in the support amount. File promptly: modifications usually apply only from the filing date, and past-due support generally can't be reduced retroactively.

Enforcement

State child support agencies can enforce orders through income withholding, interception of tax refunds, liens, driver's and professional licence suspension, passport denial and contempt of court. These services are available to parents at little or no cost.

How long support lasts

Typically until the child turns 18 or finishes high school, with variations by state and exceptions for children with disabilities. Some states allow support for college expenses.

Frequently asked questions

Can parents agree to no child support?

Parents can propose an agreement, but the court must approve it, and courts protect the child's right to support. An informal deal does not override a court order.

Does child support change if I remarry?

A new spouse's income usually doesn't count directly toward your obligation, though rules vary.

What if the paying parent lives in another state?

Interstate enforcement is handled under the Uniform Interstate Family Support Act, which every state has adopted.

When to call a lawyer

Consult a family lawyer if income is hidden or self-employed, there's a large arrears balance, or a parent is trying to impute or reduce income strategically.
